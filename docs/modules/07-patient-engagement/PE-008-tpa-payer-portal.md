## 1. Purpose

PE-008 replaces the fax, the email thread and the payer's own clunky portal with a hospital-hosted, payer-scoped workspace: the TPA or insurer's own users log in, see **only their own cases**, work a pre-authorisation queue with the hospital's clinical pack in front of them, raise queries that land directly on the right person, record approvals, partial approvals and rejections with structured reasons and amounts, exchange documents without email, upload bulk settlement advices, and see the same SLA clock the hospital sees. Every action is attributed and audited on both sides — which is exactly what makes IRDAI TAT disputes, denial arguments and settlement reconciliation resolvable with evidence rather than assertion.

## 2. Users & Jobs-to-be-done

- **Payer authorisation officer** (desktop, high volume): open the pre-auth queue for this hospital, read the clinical justification and documents in one place, approve/query/reject with an amount and reason, within the IRDAI hour.
- **Payer medical officer**: review the clinical case, raise a medical query, and get the treating doctor's reply without a phone chain.
- **Payer claims processor**: work the claim queue, request deficiencies, record line-level approvals and disallowances with reasons.
- **Payer finance user**: upload a settlement advice covering 200 claims and let the system match it, rather than emailing a spreadsheet.
- **Hospital insurance desk**: stop chasing by phone; see exactly who at the payer is holding which case and for how long.
- **Hospital finance**: reconcile settlements the payer uploaded themselves, with TDS and disallowances already itemised.
- **DPO**: prove that every clinical document a payer saw was covered by consent and was the minimum necessary.

## 3. Core Workflows

### 3.1 Payer onboarding & user management

1. The hospital's insurance lead activates the portal for an empanelled payer/TPA (EN-002 `ins_empanelments` must be `active`) and records the **data-processing/confidentiality terms** (IRDAI TPA regulations + a DPDP-compliant sharing agreement) — the portal cannot be activated without them on file.
2. **Payer admin** is invited by email (single-use, expiring); they create sub-users with roles enum(payer_admin/authorisation_officer/medical_officer/claims_processor/finance_user/read_only), each scoped to the payer (and optionally to branches or lines of business). Large payers may use **SSO** (`payer.sso`, OIDC/SAML via EN-025) with JIT provisioning.
3. MFA is mandatory, sessions are short and device-listed, an IP allowlist is optional, and every login and record view is audited and visible in the hospital's payer-access report.

### 3.2 Pre-authorisation queue (`payer.preauth_queue`)

1. The payer's queue shows only their own requests from RC-002: patient (name, age, sex, policy number — the minimum identity needed), hospital, ROHINI id, admission date, diagnosis (ICD-10), planned procedure with the payer/ROHINI code, requested amount, the estimate breakdown (RC-008), expected LOS and room class, the doctor's signed clinical justification, and the document checklist — each item viewable inline, watermarked with the payer user's name and timestamp.
2. **Actions**: Approve (amount, validity, approved room class, co-pay/deductible, non-payables list, remarks, approval letter upload or `payer.esign_letters` generation), Partially approve (amount + structured reasons), Query (category medical/document/policy/billing, text, attachments, expected response time), Reject (structured reason from the shared taxonomy mapped to RC-004 plus the payer's own wording — IRDAI requires reasons in writing), or Reassign internally.
3. Every action writes straight into RC-002's state machine with the payer user's identity, so the hospital sees "Approved ₹1,20,000 by Priya S (Star Health) at 14:32" rather than an unattributed letter. **Enhancement** and **extension** requests appear in the same queue with the interim bill and updated notes attached.
4. **Final authorisation** at discharge appears as a distinct, time-critical tab with the IRDAI 3-hour clock visible to _both_ sides.

### 3.3 Claim queue (`payer.claim_queue`)

- Submitted claims (RC-001) appear with the claim pack (bookmarked PDF or item-wise viewer), itemised bill with payer codes, discharge summary, reports, implant invoices with UDI, and the pre-auth chain. The processor can: acknowledge, request a deficiency (document/coding/medical/policy), record **line-level** approvals and disallowances with reason codes, approve, partially approve, or reject. Line-level entry is the default because a lump-sum deduction without reasons is the single biggest cause of downstream disputes (RC-004 §3.9).
- Resubmissions arrive as new pack versions with a diff of what changed, so the processor does not re-read 200 pages.

### 3.4 Document exchange (`payer.document_exchange`)

- Two-way, in-portal, no email: the hospital uploads pack documents and query replies; the payer uploads approval/denial letters, settlement advices and their own forms. Every document carries type, uploader identity, timestamp, page count and sha256; downloads are watermarked with the payer user's name, the case reference and the timestamp; access is logged and surfaced to the patient's access log (PE-001) as a payer disclosure. **Minimum-necessary** is enforced: the payer sees only the documents on the agreed checklist for that case, and sensitive-category documents (psychiatry, HIV/STI, fertility, oncology genetics) require an explicit consent tick recorded at pack assembly.

### 3.5 Query & response loop

- The payer raises a query with a category; the hospital's routing sends it to the right owner (medical → treating doctor, document → insurance desk/MRD, billing → billing executive) with an internal SLA; the reply and attachments return in-portal, and the payer's decision clock (which pauses while a query is open) is displayed to both sides. Query threads are permanent, attributable and exportable — which is what settles "we asked for that three days ago" arguments.

### 3.6 Bulk settlement upload (`payer.bulk_settlement`)

1. The payer's finance user uploads a settlement advice (XLSX/CSV in the payer's own layout, mapped once via a saved template, or a structured file) with: claim number, hospital reference, claimed, approved, deducted with reason codes, TDS, net paid, UTR and payment date.
2. The system validates, **auto-matches** to claims (claim number exact → hospital reference → policy + amount within tolerance), shows a match grid to the payer, and on submission creates a settlement record in EN-002 §3.7 for the hospital's finance team to allocate and post to NC-009. Unmatched lines are flagged to both sides rather than silently dropped.
3. Deduction reasons entered here map through RC-004's payer-wording map, so the hospital's denial analytics work without re-keying.

### 3.7 SLA dashboards (`payer.sla_dashboard`) — shown to both sides

- The payer sees their own performance at this hospital: median and 90th-percentile time to first decision (against the IRDAI 1-hour reference), final-authorisation time (against 3 hours), query round-trip time, cases pending beyond SLA by age, and volume trends. The hospital sees the same numbers plus its own submission TAT and document-completeness rate.
- Publishing a payer's SLA performance _to the payer_ is deliberate: it converts a subjective complaint into a shared, evidenced metric, and it is the basis for the payer scorecard used in contract renegotiation (RC-004 §10).

### 3.8 Exceptions

- **Payer with an API/NHCX**: the portal is unnecessary for transactions; PE-008 remains available for document exchange and dashboards, and the routing table (RC-001 §3.5) decides. Portal and API must never both be authoritative for the same case — the portal shows "handled via NHCX" for API-routed cases.
- Empanelment expires or is suspended → payer users retain read access to historical cases but cannot act on new ones; the hospital's desk is notified.
- Payer user leaves the TPA → the payer admin disables them; the hospital can force-disable an entire payer account in one action (with a reason) if a security or contractual issue arises.
- Consent absent or withdrawn for payer sharing → the case's documents are not visible; the payer sees an honest state and the hospital's desk is prompted to obtain consent.
- Dispute over what was sent/seen → the transmission and access logs, with checksums and watermarks, are exportable as evidence.
- Portal outage → the hospital falls back to email/portal-of-the-payer channels (RC-001 transmissions), and nothing is lost.

## 5. Business Rules & Validations

- **Strict payer scoping**: a payer user can see only cases where `ins_cases.payer_id` (or `tpa_id`) matches their profile, and only within their branch scope. A direct request for another payer's case returns **404** (never 403 — the existence of another payer's case is itself information). Cross-payer access is covered by an automated test in CI.
- **Consent gate on every clinical document**: the request-time check verifies an `insurance_sharing` consent covering this case; sensitive-category documents require the explicit additional consent recorded at pack assembly and `sensitive_categories_allowed` on the payer profile. Withdrawal takes effect on the next request.
- **Minimum necessary**: the payer sees only the document types on the agreed checklist for that payer; a document outside the checklist is not listed, not linked and not downloadable.
- **Every decision needs structure**: an approval requires an amount and validity; a partial approval requires per-line or per-reason amounts; a rejection requires a reason code (mapped to RC-004) _and_ the payer's written wording — an unstructured rejection cannot be saved, because IRDAI requires reasons in writing and the hospital's analytics require codes.
- **Attribution is mandatory**: every action stores the payer user, timestamp, IP hash and correlation id; the hospital's UI displays the payer user's name and designation on every state change. Anonymous payer action is not possible.
- **Portal actions are authoritative only for portal-routed cases**: if the payer's routing is NHCX or an API adapter, the portal is read-only for those cases and displays the channel, so two channels can never disagree about state.
- **SLA clocks are computed once** in `payer_sla_snapshots` and rendered identically to both sides; the payer decision clock pauses between `query_raised` and `query_replied`, and this pause logic is stated on screen so nobody has to reverse-engineer it.
- **Settlement uploads never post to the ledger directly**: they create a settlement record for the hospital's finance team to allocate and post (segregation of duties — a payer must not be able to write to the hospital's GL).
- Empanelment status governs action rights: expired/suspended → historical read-only.
- MFA mandatory; invitations single-use and expiring; sessions short; optional IP allowlist; rate limits and bot protection at EN-026; the hospital can disable a payer account or a payer user immediately with a recorded reason.
- Watermarking: every document view/download carries the payer user's name, case reference and timestamp — this materially reduces onward leakage and makes any leaked document traceable.
- No payer user ever holds a hospital permission (`patient.*`, `billing.*`, `clinical.*`); their entire capability set is the `payer.*` namespace, asserted by a permission-catalogue test.

## 7. Domain Events (outbox)

- `payer.portal.activated|suspended` {payer, agreement_ref} → EN-002, desk notification.
- `payer.user.invited|activated|disabled` → security monitoring, audit.
- `payer.case.assigned` {payer_user} → hospital desk visibility ("held by Priya S since 11:20").
- `payer.preauth.decision.recorded` {action, amount, valid_till, reason_code, payer_user} → **RC-002 state machine**, IP-005 credit limit, patient notification (PE-001/EN-009), RC-004 if denied.
- `payer.query.raised` {category, text} → RC-002/RC-001 query routing to doctor/desk/MRD with internal SLA.
- `payer.claim.decision.recorded` {lines[], reasons[]} → RC-001, RC-004, RC-005.
- `payer.document.uploaded` {kind} → desk task (approval letter received, denial letter received).
- `payer.document.accessed` {document, payer_user, consent_ref} → **patient access log (PE-001)**, DPO report.
- `payer.settlement.uploaded|submitted` {rows, net, utr} → EN-002 settlement record, finance task, RC-005.
- `payer.sla.snapshot.computed` {payer, period, metrics} → both dashboards, RC-004 payer scorecard.
- `payer.consent.blocked_access` {case, document} → desk task to obtain consent, DPO log.
- Consumes: `preauth.submitted|enhancement_requested|final_authorisation_requested` (RC-002 → payer queue), `claim.submitted|resubmitted` (RC-001), `preauth.query.replied` / `claim.query.replied` (hospital replies), `insurance.empanelment.expiring|expired` (access change), `consent.withdrawn` (immediate access change).

## 8. Screens (external, desktop-first — payer staff work on desktops all day)

- **Payer queue** (desktop): tabs _New pre-auth_, _Enhancements_, _Final authorisation (3 h clock)_, _Queries awaiting hospital_, _Claims to process_, _Deficiencies_, _Settled_. Columns: patient (name, age/sex, policy no), hospital/branch, admission date, procedure, requested amount, **SLA countdown chip**, assigned payer user, age since receipt. Filters and saved views; bulk assign. Shortcuts: `A` approve, `Q` query, `R` reject, `D` deficiency, `↓/↑` navigate, `/` search by policy or claim number.
- **Case detail** (desktop, split view): left = structured summary (policy, plan, sum insured, diagnosis with ICD-10, procedure with payer code, requested amount, estimate breakdown, expected LOS, room class, treating doctor and their signed justification); right = document viewer with the checklist (each item watermarked on open) and a page-thumbnail rail; bottom = the action bar (Approve with amount/validity/room class/co-pay/non-payables, Partial with reasons, Query with category, Reject with reason code + written wording). A permanent banner shows the SLA remaining and the pause state if a query is open.
- **Query thread**: chronological, attributable, with attachments and who at the hospital is responsible; the decision clock's pause state is explicit.
- **Claim processing**: pack viewer with bookmarks, itemised bill grid with payer codes, and a **line-decision grid** (claimed / approved / disallowed / reason) with keyboard-fast entry, running totals and a variance indicator; resubmission diff view.
- **Settlement upload**: file upload with a saved column mapper, validation report, auto-match grid (matched / ambiguous / unmatched with suggestions), totals reconciliation (gross = deducted + TDS + net), and Submit — after which the hospital's finance team allocates and posts.
- **SLA dashboard**: first-decision time distribution against the IRDAI 1-hour reference, final-auth against 3 hours, query round-trip, pending-over-SLA by age bucket, monthly trend, and a comparison of this payer's own users. Presented factually, not accusingly — the point is joint operational improvement.
- **Users & security**: sub-user management with roles and scope, MFA status, session list, IP allowlist (if configured), and **"my organisation's access log"** so the payer's own compliance team can see what their staff viewed.
- **Hospital-side payer console** (desktop, insurance desk): which cases are held by which payer user and for how long, payer SLA scorecards, payer access report for the DPO, document-checklist configuration per payer, force-disable controls.
- WCAG 2.2 AA; dense, keyboard-driven layouts suited to high-volume processing; no mobile optimisation is promised beyond a readable tablet view.

## 9. Integrations

- **EN-002** (payer/empanelment master, settlement records, desk workflow), **RC-002** (pre-auth state machine — every portal decision is an RC-002 transition, never a parallel state), **RC-001** (claim packs, transmissions, decisions; **NHCX is the strategic replacement** for portal keying and the routing table decides per payer), **RC-004** (reason taxonomy and payer wording map), **RC-005** (settlement effect on AR), **NC-009** (posting, TDS — by the hospital's finance team, never by the payer), **EN-028** (consent gate), **EN-025** (SSO), **EN-026** (rate limits, bot protection), **EN-032/EN-009** (invitations and notifications), **EN-016** (`payer.esign_letters` where a payer wants to sign approval letters in-portal), **EN-024** (audit).
- Fallbacks: portal down → the hospital's normal channels (email/payer portal) continue via RC-001 transmissions; payer's own systems unavailable → they can still act here; settlement file malformed → row-level error report, nothing submitted.

## 10. Reports & Analytics

- **For the payer**: their own SLA performance, volumes, approval/partial/rejection mix, average approved vs requested, query rate, and processing time by their own users.
- **For the hospital**: payer scorecard (decision TAT vs IRDAI reference, unstated-reason rate, query rate per 100 cases, appeal overturn rate from RC-004, settlement timeliness and shortfall rate), which payer users are fast or slow, portal adoption per payer (portal vs email vs NHCX), document access patterns, and the **payer access report** for the DPO (documents viewed, by whom, under which consent).
- Reconciliation: settlements uploaded via portal vs received by bank; unmatched rows aged.
- Read models: `analytics.mv_payer_sla`, `mv_payer_scorecard`, `mv_payer_portal_adoption`, `mv_payer_access_audit`.

## 11. Notifications

- **Payer users** (email + in-app, per preference): new pre-auth request awaiting decision, enhancement received, **final authorisation requested (3-hour clock)**, claim submitted, hospital replied to your query, settlement upload processed, case reassigned to you, SLA breach on a case you hold.
- **Hospital desk**: payer decision recorded (approved/partial/denied with amount), query raised (routed to doctor/desk/MRD), approval or denial letter uploaded, settlement advice uploaded, payer user inactive on an aged case, consent-blocked access attempt.
- **Hospital finance**: settlement upload ready for allocation, totals mismatch in an uploaded advice.
- **DPO/Admin**: unusual payer access pattern (bulk downloads, out-of-hours access), payer profile suspended, sharing agreement expiring.
- **Patient** (PE-001/EN-009, via RC-002): pre-auth approved/queried/denied — the patient learns the outcome from the hospital, not from silence.

## 12. Permissions (RBAC keys)

External (role 62, ABAC scoped to `payer_id` and branch scope): `payer.self`, `payer.user.manage` (payer_admin only), `payer.preauth.list|read|decide`, `payer.claim.list|read|decide`, `payer.query.raise`, `payer.document.read|upload`, `payer.case.assign`, `payer.settlement.upload`, `payer.dashboard.read`.
**No payer role holds any hospital permission** — no `patient.*`, `clinical.*`, `billing.*`, `lab.*`, `rad.*` — asserted by a permission-catalogue test; clinical document access exists only through the consent-checked, checklist-filtered case endpoints.
Hospital-side: `insurance.portal.configure` (Insurance lead + DPO — activation, document checklist, sensitive-category permission), `insurance.portal.users.disable` (Insurance lead, IT — emergency force-disable), `insurance.portal.access_report.read` (**DPO, Admin, Auditor**), `insurance.portal.scorecard.read` (Insurance lead, Finance, Admin).

## 13. Non-functional

- **Volumes** (2000-bed hospital): 15–40 empanelled payers/TPAs, 100–400 payer users, 90 pre-auth requests/day, 60–120 claims/day, 250–350 live cases, settlement advices of up to 500 rows; peak payer activity concentrated 10:00–18:00 on weekdays.
- **Performance**: queue p95 < 200 ms over 500 open cases with SLA computation (SLA due times stored, not computed per request); case detail with 40 documents < 400 ms; document open (watermark applied) < 1.5 s for a 5 MB PDF; settlement upload of 500 rows validated and matched < 60 s async; decision write < 250 ms including the RC-002 transition.
- **Security** (external, PHI-bearing surface — the highest-risk portal in the product): mandatory MFA, short sessions with device list, optional IP allowlist, single-use expiring invitations, strict `payer_id` row filtering verified by automated cross-payer tests, request-time consent checks (never cached authorisation), watermarked documents, download rate limits and bulk-download alerting, no PHI in URLs or notification bodies, annual penetration test, and an immediate force-disable control.
- **Auditability**: `payer_actions` and `payer_document_access` are append-only and exportable as a signed evidence pack for IRDAI TAT disputes, denial arguments and DPDP investigations.
- **Availability**: portal downtime must not stop cashless care — RC-001/RC-002 fall back to email/portal channels automatically, and the payer can still be reached by phone.
- **Accessibility/i18n**: WCAG 2.2 AA; English (payer working language) with dense keyboard-first layouts.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria

1. Given a payer user, when they list cases, then only their own payer's cases within their branch scope are returned, and a direct request for another payer's case returns **404** (cross-payer test in CI).
2. Given a payer profile without a signed sharing/DPDP agreement on file, then the portal cannot be activated.
3. Given a case without an `insurance_sharing` consent, when the payer opens it, then clinical documents are not listed or downloadable, an honest state is shown, and a task is created for the hospital desk to obtain consent.
4. Given consent is withdrawn while the payer is logged in, then their next document request fails immediately (no cached authorisation) and the block is logged.
5. Given a psychiatry report in a claim pack, then it is visible to the payer only if the sensitive-category consent tick was recorded at pack assembly and the payer profile permits it.
6. Given a payer opens or downloads a document, then it is watermarked with their name, the case reference and the timestamp, the access is logged, and it appears in the patient's access log in PE-001.
7. Given a payer approves a pre-auth for ₹1,20,000 valid till 25-Aug in a semi-private room, then RC-002 transitions accordingly within 5 s, IP-005's credit limit updates, and the hospital sees the payer user's name and designation on the transition.
8. Given a payer attempts to reject without a structured reason code, then the action is rejected with a validation error requiring both the code and the written wording.
9. Given a partial approval, then per-line or per-reason amounts are mandatory and the totals must reconcile to the approved amount.
10. Given a query is raised at 11:00 and answered at 15:00, then the payer's decision clock excludes those four hours, and both dashboards show the identical computed figure from the same snapshot.
11. Given a final authorisation request, then the 3-hour IRDAI clock is displayed to both sides and escalation notifications fire per configuration.
12. Given a settlement advice of 500 rows is uploaded, then rows are validated, auto-matched by claim number/hospital reference/amount tolerance, unmatched rows are flagged to both sides, and submission creates a settlement record for the hospital's finance team — **the payer cannot post to the hospital's ledger**.
13. Given deduction reasons in an uploaded advice, then they map through RC-004's payer wording map and appear in the hospital's denial analytics without re-keying.
14. Given a payer routed to NHCX, then their portal shows those cases as read-only with the channel indicated, and no portal action can create a conflicting state.
15. Given an empanelment expires, then payer users retain read access to historical cases but cannot act on new ones.
16. Given a payer user is force-disabled by the hospital, then their sessions are revoked immediately and the reason is audited.
17. Given a payer user downloads 50 documents in 10 minutes, then a bulk-download alert reaches the DPO and IT.
18. Given a dispute about whether a document was shared, then the access log with checksums, watermarks and timestamps is exportable as a signed evidence pack.
19. Given a payer user, then they hold no hospital permission of any kind (permission-catalogue test).
20. Given 500 open cases, then the queue loads p95 < 200 ms and the SLA chips match a recomputation from the stored due times.

## 15. Enhancements / Later phases

- Origin: architect-added (M), completing EN-002's "empanelment management portal for payers" enhancement and providing the human counterpart to RC-001's NHCX automation.
- Later: **NHCX-first operation** — as payers join the National Health Claims Exchange, the portal becomes a document-exchange and dashboard surface while transactions flow as FHIR (RC-001), and PE-008's routing table manages the transition payer by payer; payer-side **e-signature** on approval letters (`payer.esign_letters`); structured **medical-necessity dialogue** templates so a medical officer's query and the doctor's reply are comparable across cases; auto-population of the payer's decision from their own core system via EN-017 adapters; **shared SLA arbitration reports** accepted by both parties for IRDAI reporting; payer-side analytics on their own portfolio at this hospital (length of stay, package mix) strictly at aggregate level with k-anonymity if patient-level inference is possible; scheme payers (RC-007) onboarded where state agencies agree; automated claim-pack indexing so a processor lands on the exact page a query refers to (AI-003); fraud-signal sharing within legal limits and with governance; and a payer satisfaction loop so the hospital learns which of its packs are hardest to process.

## 16. Open Questions for the Hospital

1. Which payers/TPAs would actually use a hospital-hosted portal, and which insist on their own? (Most will insist on theirs — the honest use case is document exchange, query threads and SLA evidence.)
2. Do you have NHCX participant credentials, and which payers are live on it? That determines how much of this portal is transitional.
3. What document checklist may each payer see? Do any payers demand documents you consider excessive (a DPDP minimum-necessary question)?
4. Do you capture `insurance_sharing` consent from every insured patient today, and is it itemised for sensitive categories?
5. Who at the hospital may activate a payer portal profile and configure the checklist — insurance lead alone, or with the DPO?
6. What decision SLA does each payer contractually commit to, beyond the IRDAI 1-hour/3-hour references?
7. Would payers upload settlement advices here, and in what format? Can we see samples for the column mappers?
8. Do you want payer SLA performance shared _with the payer_? (Recommended — it converts arguments into evidence, but confirm the commercial appetite.)
9. Should approval/denial letters be e-signed in-portal, or will payers continue to upload PDFs?
10. Who is your DPO, and how often should the payer access report be reviewed?
11. Under what circumstances would you force-disable a payer account, and who authorises it?
12. Do any government schemes (RC-007) want to use this portal, or will they remain on their own systems?
